Your feed is mostly things you never asked for. This gives you back the part
you chose.


WHAT IT DOES

Feed Filter looks at every post in your Facebook home feed and works out one
thing: did you choose this?

A post from a friend you added, or a group you joined, is something you asked
for. A page you have never followed, a "suggested for you" post, an ad - is
not. You decide what happens to the rest: leave it alone, grey it out, or hide
it completely.

It also counts, so you can see what your feed is actually made of. On the feed
this was built against, roughly one post in six came from a friend or a joined
group. The rest was chosen for you.


WHY IT KEEPS WORKING

Most feed cleaners run from a blocklist: hide anything labelled "Sponsored",
hide "Suggested for you", hide Reels, hide "People you may know". That is a
race against a team shipping changes daily, and it loses ground every month.

Feed Filter asks the opposite question. Instead of "is this one of the things
Facebook injects?", it asks "is this someone I picked?" - checked against a
list you build yourself from your own friends and groups pages.

The difference is that new kinds of injected content need no new rule.

It also does not need your Facebook set to English. The Follow and Join buttons
are recognised in around twenty European languages, and the sponsored and
suggested labels in a dozen, with accents folded away so spelling variations do
not matter.


IT ERRS TOWARDS LEAVING THINGS ALONE

Before anything is dimmed or hidden, something has to actively show you are not
connected to the source: a Follow button, a Join button, a "Suggested" label,
or an ad marker.

Not being on your list is not enough on its own. A page you already follow
never shows a Follow button - precisely because you follow it - so it stays.
The cost of that choice is that some unwanted posts survive; the benefit is
that a post you did want is not silently removed before you ever see it.

Friends and groups you joined are never touched, under any setting.


SETTING IT UP

Two pages, scrolled to the bottom once each:

  * facebook.com/friends/list  ("All friends")
  * facebook.com/groups/joins  ("Your groups")

The extension reads the rows as they appear. It does not scroll for you, open
pages in the background, or collect anything you have not opened yourself. The
popup shows a running count so you can watch it work.

Worth repeating occasionally - a group you joined last month is not on a list
captured before you joined it.


CHECKING IT IS RIGHT

Switch on the outline overlay and every post gets a colour: green for friends,
blue for groups you joined, orange for pages and strangers, red for ads, grey
for anything it could not read.

Look at a few posts you recognise. Counts can be confidently wrong, and this is
the only honest way to know it is working on your feed.

The popup also reports its own health. If the share of unreadable posts climbs,
it says so rather than quietly producing a wrong number.


OPTING OUT PERMANENTLY (OPTIONAL, OFF BY DEFAULT)

Hiding a post only affects your browser. If you would rather a source stopped
appearing at all, two optional features use Facebook's own "Hide all from..."
setting:

  * a button on unchosen posts, which acts only when you click it
  * an automatic sweep, which applies it to unchosen sources as they appear,
    without asking each time

Please read this part before switching either on:

  * It changes a setting on your Facebook account, not just in your browser.
  * It therefore applies everywhere you use Facebook, including your phone.
  * It is permanent until you reverse it on Facebook itself, under
    Settings -> News Feed -> Reconnect.
  * Facebook keeps no list of what you have hidden. The extension keeps a local
    one, so you can see what was done.

Both are off unless you turn them on. Ads are deliberately left out of the
automatic sweep - there is an endless supply of advertisers, so hiding them one
at a time achieves nothing.


PRIVACY

Nothing is collected. There is no server, and the extension makes no network
requests of any kind - not to me, not to anyone.

  * No analytics, no tracking, no telemetry, no cookies.
  * Your friends and groups list, your counts and your settings are stored in
    your browser's local storage on your own device. They are never synced and
    never transmitted.
  * The text of posts is read to classify them and then discarded. It is never
    stored.
  * It is only active on facebook.com, and never reads your credentials or your
    messages.
  * It contains no third-party code and loads nothing at runtime.

Uninstalling deletes everything it has stored.


HONEST LIMITATIONS

  * Facebook reshapes its markup constantly, and when it does this will break.
    The popup tells you when that happens instead of silently reporting
    nonsense, and fixing it means editing one file.
  * It works on the desktop web only. A browser extension cannot reach the
    phone app.
  * It counts the home feed, not profiles or group pages - those are content
    you navigated to on purpose.
  * Building the index takes a couple of minutes of scrolling. Without it,
    nothing works.


OPEN SOURCE

Every line is readable, there is no build step, no bundler and no dependencies
of any kind. Each claim on this page can be checked against the source, and the
test suite runs in your own browser.
